## 4. Core Product Vision

The application must help me develop a sustainable system for achieving long-term targets.

The application should provide five continuous capabilities:

**PLAN → EXECUTE → REMEMBER → ANALYZE → ADAPT**

### PLAN
Convert aspirations into milestones, weekly objectives, and achievable daily tasks.

### EXECUTE
Show clear priorities, available capacity, task progress, and the next best action.

### REMEMBER
Deliver timely reminders about scheduled tasks, deadlines, daily intentions, and personal motivation.

### ANALYZE
Measure consistency, completion, estimation accuracy, recurring interruptions, and progress toward outcomes.

### ADAPT
Identify the likely reasons for missed targets and help create a realistic recovery plan.

The application should be supportive but honest. It must encourage discipline without creating shame, excessive pressure, or unrealistic expectations.

## 6. Feature Two: Daily Notification and Reminder Engine

Build a dependable reminder system with user-configurable schedules.

### Reminder categories

Support:
1. Morning planning reminder.
2. Scheduled task reminders.
3. High-priority unfinished task reminders.
4. Study and practice session reminders.
5. Long-term goal check-ins.
6. Evening review reminder.
7. Weekly review reminder.
8. Upcoming deadline warnings.
9. Recovery reminders for rescheduled tasks.
10. Optional motivational messages.

### Reminder settings

Provide configuration for:
- Enable or disable individual reminder types.
- Preferred reminder time.
- Selected weekdays.
- Time zone, defaulting to `Asia/Kolkata`.
- Quiet hours.
- Snooze duration.
- Reminder lead time before a deadline.
- Maximum reminders per day.
- Notification channels.
- Notification history.
- Retry policy for transient delivery failures.

Avoid duplicate notifications for the same task, event, and scheduled occurrence.

Changing a task's schedule must update its future reminders.

Completed, deleted, or cancelled tasks must not continue generating reminders.

### Notification channels

Design a pluggable notification interface.

Implement the most reliable local notification channel supported by the actual runtime environment, such as desktop notifications or browser notifications, depending on feasibility.

Potential channels:
- In-app notification center.
- Browser notifications with permission.
- Desktop notifications through a supported operating-system integration.
- Optional email or messaging integrations in a future version.

Do not assume that Streamlit alone can deliver notifications while its application is closed.

Document the distinction between:
- Notifications while the application is open.
- Notifications while the browser is in the background.
- Notifications while the browser is closed.
- Notifications when the application server is stopped.

If persistent background delivery is required, use a separate, lightweight notification worker or operating-system scheduler. It must be optional, configurable, and appropriate for the deployment environment.

Do not run a background scheduler every time Streamlit reruns or starts multiple duplicate workers.

### Daily reminder workflow

**Morning**
- Show today's priorities.
- Display the top three outcomes for the day.
- Identify overdue tasks and approaching deadlines.
- Show planned workload versus available time.
- Ask for a short daily intention.

**During the day**
- Remind me about selected tasks.
- Provide snooze, complete, reschedule, and dismiss actions where appropriate.
- Avoid repeatedly notifying me about completed tasks.

**Evening**
- Summarize completed and pending tasks.
- Ask why important tasks were missed.
- Compare planned and actual time.
- Help select recovery actions.
- Generate a realistic starting plan for tomorrow.

If the app was offline, handle missed reminders sensibly. Do not flood me with every expired notification when it starts again.

## 9. Feature Five: Why Am I Not Getting Results? — Root-Cause Analysis

This is one of the most important features of the application.

Create a dedicated Performance Diagnosis and Reflection section.

The system must explain not only what happened, but also what evidence suggests contributed to the result.

### Collect useful evidence

Analyze:
- Tasks planned versus completed.
- Completion percentages by category and priority.
- Planned time versus actual time.
- Tasks repeatedly postponed.
- Deadlines missed.
- Workload exceeding available capacity.
- Goals receiving insufficient attention.
- Days with no meaningful progress.
- Recurring interruptions and blockers.
- Task estimation errors.
- Trends in self-reported energy, mood, or focus, if enabled.
- Changes in consistency over time.
- Recovery actions and whether they helped.

### Root-cause categories

Allow me to classify a missed task as:
- Unrealistic planning.
- Poor time estimation.
- Unexpected work.
- Distraction.
- Lack of clarity.
- Missing prerequisite or skill.
- Low energy or inadequate rest.
- Avoidance or procrastination.
- Competing priorities.
- External dependency.
- Technical issue.
- Other.

These are reflection categories, not automatic diagnoses of my personality or mental health.

### Diagnostic report

For each review period, show:

1. **Observed result:** What happened?
2. **Evidence:** Which recorded metrics support the observation?
3. **Likely contributing factors:** What patterns may explain the result?
4. **Confidence:** Is the conclusion strongly supported or only a possibility?
5. **Missing information:** What additional information would improve the analysis?
6. **Corrective action:** What should change?
7. **Next experiment:** What small adjustment should I test?
8. **Review date:** When should I evaluate whether it worked?

Example:

"Your planned workload exceeded your recorded available capacity on four of the last seven days. On those days, completion was lower than your weekly average. Consider reducing planned workload by 20% for the next week and comparing the results."

This example must only be generated when the underlying records support it.

Do not fabricate statistics or state that one factor caused an outcome when the data only indicates correlation.

Avoid guilt-inducing messages. Use evidence-based language such as "possible bottleneck," "observed pattern," and "suggested experiment."
